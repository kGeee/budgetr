# Short #1 — "Netflix went up 35% and told nobody"

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Platform | TikTok / Reels / Shorts / X |
| Length | 42s |
| Goal | Reach. Universal pain, zero finance literacy required. |
| CTA | "budgetr — link in bio" |
| Footage | Insights page, demo persona |

**This is the template.** Every other short follows the same five beats:
hook (0–3s) → the pain, specifically (3–12s) → the screen that solves it (12–32s) →
the differentiator (32–38s) → CTA (38–42s).

The hook must land in **three seconds** and must contain a number. No logo, no intro, no
"hey guys." The first frame is already the app.

---

## Script

**0:00–0:03 — HOOK**

> [VISUAL: full-screen on the alert card. Push in slightly. Text overlay:
> **"Netflix went up 35%"**]

Netflix raised your price and didn't tell you.

**0:03–0:12 — THE PAIN**

> [VISUAL: scroll a bank statement, a wall of near-identical charges.]

You wouldn't notice. It's three dollars fifty on a statement with four hundred lines.
It happens to five services at once and suddenly you're paying two hundred a year more
than last year, for exactly the same stuff.

**0:12–0:32 — THE SCREEN**

> [VISUAL: Insights page. The price-creep card fills the frame.]

So my app watches for it. Latest charge: twenty-two ninety-nine. What you'd been paying:
seventeen oh-seven. That's a thirty-five percent increase, flagged the day it happened.

> [VISUAL: scroll slowly through the other alert types, one beat each.]

Same detector catches spending spikes against your own baseline — six-point-eight times
your usual at Amazon. Duplicate charges — two identical forty-two sixty hits at the same
pharmacy, two days apart.

**0:32–0:38 — THE DIFFERENTIATOR**

> [VISUAL: Finder, the local `budgetr.db` file.]

And it does all that on your Mac. No account, no cloud, nothing uploaded — the whole
app is a file you own.

**0:38–0:42 — CTA**

> [VISUAL: Overview, clean. Text overlay: **"budgetr · runs on your Mac"**]

It's called budgetr. Buy it once, no subscription. Link in bio.

## Production notes

- **No music under the hook.** Voice cold on the first line, music in at 0:03. The
  silence is what makes people stop.
- **Numbers on screen as text overlays**, not just spoken — most views are muted.
  `$17.07 → $22.99` as a hard cut is the single most important frame in the video.
- **One screen only.** The temptation is to show the whole app. Don't — the video is
  about one alert.
- Captions burned in. Platform auto-captions mangle "budgetr" every time.

## Variants worth testing

- Swap the hook number for the **spending spike** ("You spent 6.8× your usual at Amazon") and keep everything else. Tests whether the pain or the surveillance angle
  pulls harder.
- A version that ends on the live web demo instead of pricing, for colder audiences.
